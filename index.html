Arnath THE IRREVERSIBILITY DEMON · Σ ≥ 0
Meet Arnath, the demon of irreversibility. Unlike the more famous Maxwell’s demon, Arnath loves locking doors that will never reopen. The result is Σ, the entropy produced by every real process, which is never undone (on average). Arnath helps us stay alive and the systems we study functional and interesting!

Methods and models for nonequilibrium chemistry

Designing chemistry that runs on energy

We are a theoretical chemistry group at Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz. With pen-and-paper theory, simulations and a little electrochemistry, we look for design rules for molecular systems that harvest energy and do work far from equilibrium.

Explore our research Meet Emanuele

01 – Research

Three questions drive our lab

Living cells run on molecular machines that burn fuel to move, build and sort. Chemists can now make simple artificial ones, but designing them is still mostly trial and error, because chemistry’s theoretical toolbox was built for systems at equilibrium. We build the theory and simulations to design them.

Try it: watch random motion turn into directed motion

How does a network’s structure decide what it can do?

Kept away from equilibrium, networks of reactions can pump molecules uphill, pick out the right partner and keep time, as they do in living cells. We work out which network structures give which function, and what each function costs in energy.

Functional reaction networks

Can we design a molecular motor before anyone makes it?

Biological motors turn fuel into motion and motion back into fuel; no artificial motor can do this yet. We simulate motors bead by bead to find the design rules for machines that truly transduce energy.

Molecular machines by design

What is the best way to drive chemistry with electricity?

Rapidly switching electrode polarity unlocks new reactions, but the waveforms in use are simple and symmetric. We combine reaction-network theory, simulations and experiments to design them from first principles.

Programmable electrochemistry
