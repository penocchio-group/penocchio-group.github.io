Group culture

We are just getting started, so this is a plan; the first members will help refine it.

Our mentoring compact

Clear expectations make everything easier. We will go through this list with every new member in their first week, and revisit it together once a year.

What you can expect from Emanuele

  • Regular one-to-one meetings, weekly or every two weeks, and an open door in between.
  • Honest, constructive feedback on ideas, derivations, code and writing.
  • Support to present your work at international meetings every year and to build your own network.
  • Help with fellowships, next steps and reference letters, in or out of academia.
  • Credit where it is due: you present your own work, and authorship is discussed openly and early.

What we expect from each other

  • Curiosity and ownership of your project.
  • Helping each other out: practice talks, code reviews, a second pair of eyes on a derivation.
  • Reproducible work: version-controlled code and notes someone else can follow.
  • “Expose your ignorance”: the group is a safe place to grow. Nobody has to pretend to know everything from the start, and nobody should feel ashamed to ask for help, early rather than after two weeks stuck.
  • Kindness and respect, in the group and with everyone we work with.

How we work

Theory that talks to experiments

Most of our projects have an experimental partner. You will visit their labs, look at raw data and learn to explain your work to people who do not read equations.

Rigour and honesty

We check derivations twice, test our code and say clearly what a model can and cannot do. Mistakes are normal; hiding them is not.

Open by default

Code lives in version control from day one and is released with the paper. Preprints go online when we submit, and we publish open access whenever we can.

A good place to be

An interdisciplinary, international group that welcomes, respects and supports diversity. We value each other’s time, holidays and lives outside work, and we do not tolerate harassment or discrimination.

AI as a tool, not a substitute

We are experimenting with AI as a research tool, for code, literature searches and writing, and to make theory easier to get into for newcomers. It is no substitute for understanding: you should be able to explain every equation, line of code and sentence you put your name to, because growing as a scientist is the point.

A week in the group

A typical week will have a group meeting to share progress and problems, a journal club where we read papers from both theory and experiment, one-to-one meetings, and plenty of time at the whiteboard. Several times a year we will visit collaborators or host them in Mainz, and we plan small workshops that bring our research community to us.

We will also make time for life outside work: the occasional group dinner, and invitations to go bouldering or out on a bike.

Living in Mainz

Mainz is a lively university city on the Rhine, surrounded by vineyards and about half an hour by train from Frankfurt Airport. The campus is international, English is the working language of the group, and JGU offers German courses for those who want to learn. Emanuele is learning too.

Support beyond the group

The group should be the first place to raise a problem, but never the only one. These JGU services are independent of the group.

Beyond JGU, Emanuele is a member of the German Association of University Professors and Lecturers (DHV). It supports postdocs and professors in Germany with career advice, coaching, application reviews and legal advice, and he has found it very helpful.

  • Ombudsperson for good scientific practiceNeutral, confidential advice on research integrity for every JGU member.
  • PhD representativesElected by doctoral candidates, with a voice in the Senate and faculty councils.
  • Gutenberg Council for Academic CareersAdvises the university board on working conditions for doctoral candidates and postdocs.
  • Gutenberg AcademyCareer advice, workshops, mentoring and grants for doctoral candidates and postdocs.
  • Conflict counsellingConfidential, independent help with conflicts at work, for JGU employees (site in German).
  • Anti-Discrimination OfficeSupport, also anonymous, if you experience or witness discrimination; part of the Equality and Diversity Office.
  • Mental Health ServicesFree, confidential counselling and short-term therapy for JGU students.
  • Family Services CenterAdvice for parents and carers, in person or online.
  • Welcome Center for International ScholarsPractical help for international researchers and their families settling in Mainz.
  • University sportsSports courses for students and staff (site in German).
